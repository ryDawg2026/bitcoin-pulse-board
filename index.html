How it works

Plain-English version
  1. Data. Your browser asks free, keyless public APIs of many exchanges and price sites for BTC/USD (or BTC/USDT) 15-minute candles and spot prices. Only sources that actually answered are used; the table shows which ones did.
  2. Consensus. For each 15-minute candle, the median open and close across sources is the “consensus” — one bad feed can’t move it.
  3. Call. At each :00 / :15 / :30 / :45 (your local time) the engine looks only at candles that are already closed, scores seven signals, and locks an UP or DOWN call for the next 15 minutes. Locked calls are never edited.
  4. Grade. At the next boundary the call is graded against the consensus candle: close above open = UP, below = DOWN, exactly equal = push (ignored).
  5. Backfill. This page only runs while it’s open. On load it replays the engine over up to the last 12 hours of history it missed and grades those too, tagged backfilled. Only live calls were actually made before their window started.
  6. Bets. Bet play money on the open call at even payout. Bets settle automatically when the call is graded. Everything is saved in this browser’s localStorage only — no accounts, no servers, no ads, no tracking.

Signal weights: 15m EMA trend 20% · 1h EMA trend 15% · RSI(14) extremes 15% · momentum (1–3 candles) 15% · USD-vs-USDT premium 15% · volume-weighted direction 10% · source agreement 10%. Score = Σ weight × vote (each vote −1…+1). Call = sign of the score. Confidence = 50 + 40·min(1,|score|/0.5) + 9·(share of sources whose last candle agrees), capped at 99 — a rough strength gauge, not a calibrated probability. Short-term BTC moves are close to a coin flip; expect the record to hover near 50%.

Disclaimer: For fun only. Not financial advice. Play money only — no real money, exchange accounts, or orders are involved. Predictions are experimental and often wrong. Data comes from third-party public APIs and may be delayed, wrong or unavailable.
