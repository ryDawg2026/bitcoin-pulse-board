🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Meet the callers · Dash, Zippy, Mellow, Ripple, Sage and Flippy · each on their own beat · paper only, no money, no orders

Every 15-minute window, we take turns: Dash first (minute 1), then Zippy, Mellow, Ripple and Sage, each one a minute apart, three rounds of it. Flippy is the odd one out: he only speaks up at 14:15, and only if he’s sure price is about to flip across the strike against the leader. Each call is UP or DOWN, comes with a confidence score, and backs one of the Grokkys. We see exactly the same information and think equally hard; we just weigh it differently (Dash is bold and early, Zippy follows momentum, Mellow follows volume, Ripple reads the chart, Sage waits for confirmation, Flippy gambles on late flips). Scored on Kalshi’s real result. Tap “Why?” on any card for the details.

🧭 How it works

Plain-English version
  1. Data. Your browser asks free, keyless public APIs of many exchanges and price sites for BTC/USD (or BTC/USDT) 15-minute candles and spot prices. Only sources that actually answered are used; the table shows which ones did.
  2. Consensus. For each 15-minute candle, the median open and close across sources is the “consensus” — one bad feed can’t move it.
  3. Call. At each :00 / :15 / :30 / :45 (your local time) the engine looks only at candles that are already closed, scores five mean-reversion signals, and locks an UP or DOWN call for the next 15 minutes. Locked calls are never edited.
  4. Grade. At the next boundary the call is graded against the consensus candle: close above open = UP, below = DOWN, exactly equal = push (ignored).
  5. No backfill. This page only runs while it’s open and only logs calls it actually locked. Since the 2026-11-12 reset there is no replay of missed windows: the log, the Grokky tally and your play-money record all started from zero.
  6. Grokky 1 vs 2. Two paper-only pickers split every window: Grokky 1 takes the engine’s side, Grokky 2 the opposite one. Each is scored against Kalshi’s real settlement (final value vs strike), so exactly one is right per round, including “no edge” rounds.
  7. Bets. Bet play money on the open call at even payout. Bets settle automatically when the call is graded. Everything is saved in this browser’s localStorage only — no accounts, no servers, no ads, no tracking.

Engine v2 (this version). A 150-day backtest on real public candles showed the old trend-following engine called the wrong way more often than the right way (48.8% hit rate, and its “confidence” was backwards: its ≥80%-confidence calls hit 46.8%). Short-term BTC tends to give back overshoots, so v2 fades them: 1-hour vol-scaled overshoot, last-candle overshoot, RSI(14) extremes, where the last candle closed in its range, and the USD-vs-USDT premium. The coefficients were fitted only on data before 2026-09-25 and then frozen; on the 47 days after that (never used for fitting) it hit 51.8% vs 48.7% for v1. That is a small edge, not a money machine — expect long losing streaks (up to ~10 in a row in the test period). “Chance the called side is right” is the model’s own probability and is honestly only ~50–56%. “Conviction” calls are the stronger half of the signals (backtest 53.1% out-of-sample). Details and caveats: backtest/README.md.
